      Collapsing header
      
  View this demo
  
      animation-timeline: scroll() animation-range position: sticky font-variation-settings
      
  
      Idea: Ania Kubów · rebuilt
      
  
      Scroll the little page: the big header shrinks into a slim bar.
      
  
      
    
      
      
      
        
        
          Field Notes
          Issue 07 · scroll down ↓
        
      
      
      
      
        
      This header isn't watching a clock. Its animation is tied to how far this little page has been scrolled, so scrolling is the playhead.
      
        
      Scroll back up and it plays in reverse. Stop halfway and it stops halfway. No scroll listeners, no JavaScript.
      
        
      The header is sticky with a negative top, so it slides up until only a slim bar is left, and then it sticks.
      
        
      Meanwhile the title shrinks, sharpens and darkens, the subtitle fades away, and a shadow appears once the bar is floating over the text.
      
        
      That's the whole trick. Scroll up to unfold it again.

      Accordion to height: auto
      
  View this demo
  
      interpolate-size ::details-content allow-discrete details[name]
      
  
      Idea: Bramus Van Damme · rebuilt
      
  
      Open the questions: each one glides open to its natural height.
      
  
      
    
      
      
      
        Why couldn't CSS animate to auto?
        
      
          
      Transitions need two numbers to blend between, and auto isn't a number. So for years we faked it with max-height: 999px and lived with the lag.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        What changed?
        
      
          
      interpolate-size: allow-keywords tells the browser it may work out what auto is in pixels and animate to that.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        And the closing animation?
        
      
          
      The new ::details-content pseudo-element wraps everything but the summary, so it can be transitioned both ways. Sharing a name makes the panels take turns.
